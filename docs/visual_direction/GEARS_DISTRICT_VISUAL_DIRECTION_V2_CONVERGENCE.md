## 1. Converged visual thesis

### CANDIDATE — Industrial Cel-Shaded Near Future

**Short version:** grounded repurposed industrial infrastructure rendered with graphic cel-shaded clarity, anime/mecha character-design discipline, and GTA-family gameplay readability without copying GTA assets, branding, characters, locations, UI, writing or map layouts.

The world should feel like **advanced technology that arrived years ago, became infrastructure, was privatized, repaired badly, legislated repeatedly and never stopped running**.

The setting is not generic cyberpunk. Technology is physical, maintainable, numbered, patched, bought second-hand, stolen, serviced, relabeled and misunderstood.

### Style balance

- Environment massing/material logic: grounded and functional.
- Rendering: stylized, with broad value bands and selective contour emphasis.
- Humans: anime-influenced faces/proportions, strong clothing silhouettes and practical role-specific gear.
- Robots/vehicles: mecha-industrial design discipline, chunky modular forms and obvious function.
- Graphic design: major identity carrier, ranging from rigid municipal systems to loud local commerce/subculture branding.
- Mystery layer: quieter and visually rarer than ordinary infrastructure.

The target is **stylized enough to be authored and recognizable, grounded enough that crime, vehicles, pursuit and civic satire still have physical weight**.

## 2. Rendering rules

### CANDIDATE

1. **Large value groups before texture.** A building, person or vehicle must read in 3–5 major value/color masses before surface detail.
2. **Cel bands over photoreal gradients.** Use stepped/broad lighting transitions on hero assets and important architecture. Background assets can use softer shading if silhouettes remain clean.
3. **Selective contours.** Stronger dark edge treatment on characters, vehicles, interactive props and critical architectural edges. Do not outline every pipe, brick or background seam.
4. **Micro-detail is subordinate.** Scratches, fasteners, cables and labels enrich close views but cannot be the primary identity cue.
5. **Saturation is information.** Most world materials remain neutral/desaturated. Bright colors identify brands, hazards, subculture, enforcement, missions or signal/memory phenomena.
6. **Wear follows use.** Chipping, rust, grime and repairs appear at edges, fasteners, drainage paths, contact zones and replacement panels rather than as uniform procedural grunge.
7. **Matte/satin baseline.** Gloss is selective: glass, wet roads, polished contact surfaces, fresher corporate hardware.
8. **Night is practical-light first.** Sodium/amber yards, cool utility fixtures and warm storefronts dominate. Neon is commercial punctuation, not the environmental default.

## 3. Buildings / district architecture

### KEEP

- stacked 2–5-storey mixed-use industrial/residential/commercial forms;
- bolt-on balconies, stairs, utility boxes, vents, antennas, pipe runs and repaired panels;
- asymmetrical additions that still expose the original structural logic;
- warm occupied interiors against cooler exterior masses;
- strong rooftop silhouette features;
- layered old/new infrastructure rather than abandoned apocalypse ruin;
- one dominant visual idea per building cluster.

### AVOID

- generic glossy megacity towers;
- extreme density of tiny pipes and greebles;
- every facade being a hero design;
- painterly atmosphere that destroys road/route readability;
- excessive vertical occlusion from the retained 3/4 camera.

### Gameplay rule

A building can be intricate in concept art but must collapse cleanly into **major roof mass + facade block + one signature silhouette + one graphic identity cue** at gameplay distance.

## 4. Humans

### CANDIDATE

Humans use anime-influenced design without turning the world into full cartoon rendering.

- softly simplified faces with expressive eyes/brows/mouths;
- mostly believable body proportions with selective exaggeration for silhouette;
- oversized/structured outerwear, workwear, bags, helmets, boots and utility gear;
- profession/class/hustle readable before texture;
- asymmetry and personalization are common outside formal institutions;
- prosthetics/augmentations exist but are not universal;
- high-tech attachments look maintained, repaired and commercially sourced rather than magical.

### Population rule

Not everyone is a hero silhouette. Background residents use simpler variants of the same clothing/material grammar. Distinctiveness comes from occupation, neighborhood, age, class and subculture rather than constant protagonist-level accessorizing.

### Role families

- couriers / salvage runners: layered utility wear, cargo systems, weather protection;
- mechanics / workers: visible tools, aprons/vests, reinforced contact areas;
- shopkeepers / clerks: practical local clothing with brand/permit identity;
- civic field staff: standardized blocks, IDs, reflective/route coding;
- enforcement: clearer institutional silhouette and controlled asymmetry;
- memory/signal-adjacent people: quieter palette, fewer commercial marks, one unusual meaningful object rather than fantasy costume language.

---

## 5. Robots / utility machines

### CANDIDATE

- compact, charming-but-functional silhouettes;
- off-white, faded green, amber, orange, charcoal and utility gray families;
- visible maintenance panels, fasteners and replacement modules;
- one dominant sensor/face/readout feature;
- serial numbering and service labels are common;
- personality comes from proportions, gait and repair history rather than decorative anthropomorphism.

FB-13 remains the micro-scale identity anchor: worn pale shell, dark structural core, sparse signal lighting, practical asymmetry and clear evidence of continued use.

---

## 6. Vehicles

### CANDIDATE — Retro-Utility Near Future

Ordinary vehicles should look like recognizable transportation that evolved under a salvage/service economy.

**Shared rules:**
- boxy/compact or clearly role-driven silhouette;
- slightly exaggerated wheel/body proportions;
- modular external equipment, cargo, sensors or replacement panels;
- large color/value blocks readable from elevated camera;
- front/rear distinction must survive top/3/4 view;
- local wear and relivery rather than universal apocalypse damage.

### Civilian / commercial

- compact hatches, small vans, delivery pods, utility pickups and odd local service vehicles;
- simpler silhouettes than mission/enforcement vehicles;
- aftermarket decals and small-business branding provide variety.

### Scrap Hauler / recovery

- blunt high cab, long load/recovery mass, obvious tow/lift/scrap function;
- municipal and gray-market variants can share base chassis families;
- machinery should read as functional before futuristic.

### Rare corporate / premium

Cleaner surfaces, more integrated glass and quieter hardware. These should stand out precisely because the rest of Gears is more modular and exposed.

---

## 7. Courier Bike

### CANDIDATE hero recipe

- compact modular courier machine;
- short-to-medium wheelbase;
- oversized practical tires;
- low center of mass;
- rectangular central battery/body module;
- partially exposed suspension/drive hardware;
- narrow rider saddle;
- one strong rear/side cargo or signal structure;
- one asymmetric replacement/repair panel;
- clear serial/fleet markings;
- strong roof/top silhouette when seen from retained 3/4 camera.

The Courier Bike must pass a flat single-color silhouette test and remain distinguishable from civilian/enforcement bikes without relying on tiny details.

### Avoid

- sleek contemporary superbike anatomy as baseline;
- giant smooth spaceship shell;
- extreme touring-bike bulk that contradicts current nimble gameplay;
- detail density that cannot survive gameplay camera distance.

---

## 8. Municipal enforcement fleet

### CANDIDATE — Bureaucratic Pursuit Fleet

The fleet should feel **institutional first, threatening second**.

- standardized compact/wedge/boxy chassis families;
- off-white/charcoal or similarly high-contrast civic livery;
- large readable agency marks and roof identifiers;
- integrated beacons, sensors, comms and push/interception hardware;
- modest armor rather than military-APC visual language;
- clear pursuit/interceptor, patrol/inspection and support/transport subclasses;
- vehicles look fleet-purchased, serviced and repeatedly modified through procurement cycles.

Satirical target: **municipal authority weaponized through procurement**.

## 9. Signage / branding / graphic design

Graphic design is now considered a primary world identity layer.

### Four CANDIDATE families

#### A. Municipal / civic
- rigid grid;
- block/condensed sans and stencil systems;
- large numbers;
- off-white, charcoal, amber, teal;
- clipped-corner plates, arrows, route bands, permits and asset IDs;
- overbearing administrative certainty.

#### B. Commercial / neighborhood
- custom sign silhouettes;
- hand-built frames, pipe mounts and reused substrates;
- retro-future / late-90s / Y2K influence filtered through local economics;
- stronger color freedom than municipal graphics;
- one large readable store/service identity at gameplay distance.

#### C. Aftermarket / subculture
- sticker/wordmark language;
- tuner, youth, pirate-radio, streetwear and cheap-tech energy;
- vehicles, helmets, bags, crates, vending machines and shop windows;
- more playful color and typography, but still authored into repeatable fake brands.

#### D. Criminal / gray-market spoof
- near-official seals and copied civic typography;
- altered permits, counterfeit decals, overprinted labels and cloned route marks;
- businesses that appear compliant until the player understands the hustle.

### Hero sign rule

At gameplay distance, communicate in this order:

1. shape;
2. color/value;
3. icon;
4. 1–3 readable words.

Fine print is world texture, not navigation-critical information.

## 10. Props / street furniture

Reusable prop families should share the same industrial design grammar:

- utility cabinets;
- meters;
- kiosks/payment terminals;
- vending machines;
- dumpsters/bins;
- generators;
- electrical/signal boxes;
- portable barriers;
- maintenance carts;
- loading equipment;
- streetlights;
- yard service machines.

Rule: **obvious function + chunky silhouette + replaceable panel logic + serial/inspection graphics**.

Props should support district storytelling without creating high-frequency clutter along primary driving routes.

---

## 11. Landmarks / skyline

Use large infrastructure forms as navigation, not spectacle for its own sake:

- cranes;
- relay masts;
- capped stacks;
- water towers;
- gantries;
- pipe bridges;
- silos;
- substations;
- old transit structures;
- extraction hoods / service towers.

Each important intersection or neighborhood pocket should have at least one silhouette anchor visible before the player enters it.

## 12. Silent Core / Sister Kael mystery language

### CANDIDATE

Silent Core should feel like **ordinary infrastructure whose significance outlived its understood purpose**.

- repurposed telecom/relay/server/utility architecture;
- older advanced hardware physically embedded in civic infrastructure;
- stripped or deliberately removed asset plates;
- quiet negative space compared with surrounding commercial density;
- fewer signs, fewer colors, fewer props;
- evidence of ritual through maintenance, placement, repetition and human care rather than fantasy iconography;
- sparse HS-7 cyan/memory phenomena that can dominate because the surrounding environment is restrained;
- warm practical human light can coexist with cold signal events.

### Avoid

- glowing crystal shrine;
- gothic sci-fi cathedral;
- cosmic machine-god spectacle;
- generic holographic altar;
- excessive neon mysticism.

The emotional target is: **people discovered that an obsolete piece of infrastructure remembers something the city wants forgotten, and over time they learned to approach it with ritual seriousness**.

## 13. Controlled style-test gate

Before any Gears District production plan is approved, render the **same authored scene** in three treatments:

### A — Grounded stylized baseline
- reduced photorealism but softer gradients;
- minimum contour treatment;
- restrained anime influence.

### B — Industrial cel-shaded candidate — PREFERRED
- broad value bands;
- selective contours;
- anime/mecha character/vehicle design discipline;
- grounded environment proportions/material logic;
- strong signage hierarchy.

### C — Heavy anime/comic treatment
- stronger outlines;
- flatter color separation;
- more exaggerated characters/vehicles;
- intentionally tests the upper stylization boundary.

### Test scene contents

Use one Gears District industrial-commercial intersection containing:

- readable primary road + alley shortcut;
- stacked mixed-use building;
- municipal utility structure/gantry;
- local storefront signage;
- ordinary workers/civilians;
- Courier Bike;
- one civilian/utility vehicle;
- one municipal pursuit vehicle;
- one small utility robot;
- distant Silent Core/relay silhouette or signal cue;
- restrained day-to-dusk practical lighting.

### Evaluation criteria

Score each treatment 1–5 for:

1. original identity;
2. elevated-camera readability;
3. character/world cohesion;
4. vehicle silhouette clarity;
5. signage legibility;
6. mystery tone;
7. feasibility in Godot;
8. likelihood of maintaining the style across a full district.

**No treatment becomes APPROVED by attractiveness alone.** The preferred direction must win on gameplay readability, coherence and implementation sustainability.
